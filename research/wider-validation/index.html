Wider validation: detection and borders

Results from an expanded development test of the footprint pipeline. Detecting a panel and delivering its correct border are separate checks. This is a numeric research report, not a production approval.

Presence is stronger than border delivery. At the 50% overlap threshold, the original Segment Anything Model 3 regions in the final 855 retained observations match 823 reference panels, with 32 unmatched observations and 22 unmatched reference panels.

The fitted mixed output matches 822, with 33 unmatched observations and 23 unmatched reference panels. But 57 observations still have no delivered outline, so this check reuses their original detected regions. It does not establish complete border delivery.

What the overlap thresholds mean

A reference and a detection match when their shared area, divided by the area covered by either shape counted once, reaches the selected threshold. Each detection and reference can be matched only once. A 90% requirement is stricter than 50%.

50%, 75% and 90% are matching thresholds, not accuracy percentages. Passing a threshold does not prove an exact physical metal-frame border. The reference outlines are approximate author annotations, not expert measurements of every physical edge.

At 50%, 609 of 656 fully visible reference panels have a matching complete four-corner outline; 47 do not. The 656 fully visible panels are a defined subset of the 845 references. All 189 partial references remain in the overall checks. Missing geometry is a delivery failure, not an extra detected object.

A substantial overlap means the shared area exceeds 1% of the smaller delivered outline. There are 187 such pairs among available outlines. Intersections involving the 57 unresolved outlines cannot be measured. The occupied-pixel support checks and continuous outline-area checks also use slightly different boundary conventions.

Color and thermal images give different results

Each population keeps all its references. Color images use red, green and blue channels (RGB). Thermal image results are separate; complete output availability does not imply clean or correct borders.

What this test can establish

  • The earlier three-image replay reproduced the previous software outputs exactly. That is a separate reproduction check, not the 20-image score or proof of performance on new sites.
  • These 20 images are expanded development data. Overlap with checkpoint training images or previously seen sites is unknown. The older 13-image reserve was already evaluated by earlier methods, so it is not a fresh held-out validation set.
  • Matching detections, direction consistency and border extent are different questions. Partial coverage failures, unresolved deliveries and remaining overlaps prevent a finished-engine claim.
  • The separate table-first border candidate remains experimental. These numbers describe the replayed footprint pipeline and do not qualify a completed table-first system or change the automatic default.
